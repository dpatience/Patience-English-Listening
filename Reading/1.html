Social Media and Real Friendship

Read the passage carefully, then answer the questions below. Click "Check my answers" when you are finished.

Twenty years ago, if you wanted to talk to a friend, you called their house and hoped their parents did not answer the phone. Today, things are very different. With one click, you can send a message, share a photo, or see what hundreds of people are doing at that exact moment. Social media has changed the way young people communicate, but it has also created a big question: are we becoming closer to our friends, or are we actually growing further apart?

There is no doubt that social media has some clear benefits. It allows people to stay in touch with friends and family who live far away. A student who moves to another city for university can still see photos of their old classmates and comment on their posts. Social media also helps people find others who share the same interests. For example, a teenager who loves a specific type of music or a video game can join online groups and talk to people from all over the world who feel the same passion. In this way, social media can create real connections between people who might never have met in person.

However, many psychologists and teachers are worried about the effect of social media on real, face-to-face friendship. One common problem is that people often compare their lives to what they see online. When someone scrolls through photos of friends having fun at parties, traveling to beautiful places, or wearing expensive clothes, they may start to feel that their own life is boring or not good enough. This feeling is sometimes called "social comparison," and researchers say it can lead to stress, jealousy, and even sadness. The strange part is that what people post online is often not a complete picture of their life. Most people only share their happiest, most exciting moments, not their ordinary or difficult days.

Another issue is the quality of communication itself. When you send a message online, you cannot always understand the other person's tone of voice or facial expression. A joke can be misunderstood as an insult, and a short reply can be seen as anger, even if the person was simply busy. Face-to-face conversation, on the other hand, allows people to see body language, hear tone, and react immediately. Many experts believe that this kind of direct communication builds trust and understanding more effectively than typing on a screen.

There is also a growing concern about the amount of time young people spend on their phones instead of spending time with friends in person. Imagine a group of friends sitting together at a café. In the past, they would have talked, laughed, and shared stories. Today, it is common to see a group of friends sitting silently, each one looking at their own phone. This situation even has a name: "phubbing," which means ignoring the people around you because you are focused on your phone. Although everyone is physically together, they might feel emotionally distant from each other.

Despite these challenges, social media is not the enemy of friendship — it depends on how people use it. Some students use social media to plan real meetings, share study notes, or support each other during difficult times, such as exams. A quick message saying, "Good luck with your exam today!" can make someone's whole day better. The key is balance. Instead of replacing real conversations completely, social media can be used as a tool to strengthen relationships that already exist in real life.

Experts suggest a few simple habits to keep friendships healthy in the digital age. First, try to put your phone away during meals or conversations with friends, so you can give them your full attention. Second, remember that what you see online is not always the full truth about someone's life, so try not to compare yourself too much. Third, make time for real, face-to-face meetings, even if it is just once a week, because nothing can completely replace human connection.

In conclusion, social media has both positive and negative effects on friendship. It can connect people across long distances and help them find others who share their interests, but it can also create comparison, misunderstanding, and emotional distance. The most important lesson is that technology should support real relationships, not replace them. True friendship still depends on trust, honesty, and time spent together — whether that time is online or, more importantly, face-to-face.
